I needed a low background bed for a short film and couldn't find anything in the libraries that didn't sound like a library. So I took the recorder up to Hampstead Heath early, before the dog walkers, and just left it running.

You get less than you think. An hour of recording gave me maybe ninety usable seconds. Planes ruin most of it, you don't notice them standing there but the mic does. A car door two streets away sounds like it's in the room. The wind is constant and the foam only does so much.

What I kept in the end wasn't the obvious stuff. Not the birds, which were lovely but too busy to sit under dialogue. It was a long stretch of almost-nothing: a bit of distant traffic hum, leaves, one dog somewhere. Pitched down a few semitones and rolled off at the top, it became the whole base of the scene. Nobody watching would ever name it, which is the point.

The lesson I keep relearning is that field recording is mostly editing. The recording is the easy hour. The choosing is the work.
