There's a pull, when you start something, towards the newest tool. The one with the good launch video and the opinionated docs. I've followed it plenty of times and mostly regretted it.

The trouble with new tools isn't that they're bad. It's that you become their tester. You hit the rough edges first, you search for an error and find three results, two of which are the maintainer saying it'll be fixed soon. You spend your attention on the tool instead of the thing you were trying to make.

Boring tools have had that attention spent already, by thousands of people, years ago. The error you hit at midnight was hit by someone in 2019 and answered properly underneath. The behaviour is predictable. You stop thinking about the tool, which is the whole point of a tool.

I don't mean never learn anything. New things are how the boring tools of the future get made, and some of them genuinely move the floor up. I mean be honest about which project you're on. If the work is the experiment, experiment. If the work is the work, reach for the dull thing that you already know will hold.

Most of my projects are the work. So most of the time I pick the boring option, and the project ships, and nobody asks what it was built with.
