The hard part of a side project isn't starting it. Starting is the fun bit. The hard part is the long middle, where the novelty has worn off and the thing is eighty percent done and boring. Most of mine used to die there. A few habits have changed that.

The first is to always stop mid-task, never at a clean break. If I finish a session at a tidy stopping point, picking it up next week means deciding what to do next, which is enough friction to put it off. If I stop halfway through something obvious, sitting back down is easy. I just finish the sentence.

The second is to keep a single plain file of the next three things, in order. Not a board, not an app, a text file. When I open the project I don't have to think, I just do the top one. When it's done I delete the line. The good feeling of deleting a line is doing a lot of work here.

The third is to cut scope out loud. When something's dragging, I write down what I'm not going to do and why, so future me doesn't quietly try to add it back at midnight. Half my finished projects are finished because I wrote down the half I dropped.

None of this is clever. It's just lowering the activation energy of the next session, over and over, until the thing is done.
